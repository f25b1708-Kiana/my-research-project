🧮 Mathematical Growth Model

To study food security, bioscience researchers model crop growth as a Logistic Growth Curve, preventing unconstrained exponential growth by incorporating environmental carrying capacity.

Height Function:
$$f(x) = \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(x - x_0)}}$$

Calculates the cumulative height of the paddy plant at day $x$.

First Derivative (Growth Rate):
$$f'(x) = \frac{L \cdot k \cdot e^{-k(x - x_0)}}{(1 + e^{-k(x - x_0)})^2}$$

Derived using the Chain Rule and Quotient Rule. Calculates the rate of change in plant height (cm/day). Alternatively written as $f'(x) = k \cdot f(x) \left(1 - \frac{f(x)}{L}\right)$.

Model Coefficients Explained:

  • $L$: Carrying capacity parameter (maximum target plant height in cm). Dependent on rice genetics (such as MR297 characteristics).
  • $k$: Logistic growth rate coefficient. Higher values imply rapid vegetative response to nitrogen and photosynthetically active radiation (PAR).
  • $x_0$: Inflection point representing the day of maximal growth rate. At this moment, $f''(x) = 0$.

🌾 Malaysian Bioscience Application

In Malaysia's granary zones like MADA (Muda Agricultural Development Authority) and KADA (Kemubu Agricultural Development Authority), optimized yield translates directly to community livelihoods and national buffer stock preservation.

Precision Fertilizer Top Dressing

Applying nitrogen top-dressing (Urea) is most effective when the growth rate (the derivative $f'(x)$) is accelerating rapidly. Once $f'(x)$ peaks and starts to decelerate, fertilizer is shifted toward potassium and phosphorus to support panicle initiation and heading rather than leaf elongation.

Water Conservation & Carbon Footprint

When $f'(x)$ is low (early vegetative and late grain filling stages), water levels can be safely dropped in an Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) cycle. This reduces methane emissions from flooded soil while saving precious irrigation water.
